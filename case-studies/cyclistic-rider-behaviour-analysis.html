Cyclistic Rider Behaviour Analysis

Large-scale bike-share analysis comparing member and casual rider patterns across time, duration and seasonality.

Read the case study ↓Source repository ↗

The problem

How do annual members and casual bike-share riders differ by ride timing, duration and seasonality, and which conversion hypotheses merit testing?

Data & provenance

Twelve public bike-share monthly files, cleaned into approximately 5.93 million analysed rides.

Technical approach

  1. Consolidate monthly source data and validate dates, duplicates and ride durations.
  2. Prepare Parquet-ready behavioural attributes by rider type, day, hour and season.
  3. Aggregate member and casual cohorts into reproducible analytical views.
  4. Visualise time, duration and rider-mix patterns with Power BI.

Documented findings

  • Members account for 64.36% of rides and casual riders for 35.64%.
  • Average casual ride duration is 18.57 minutes versus 12.06 minutes for members.
  • Timing and seasonality patterns differ, motivating testable segmentation rather than guaranteed conversion claims.

Decision relevance

Produces behavioural evidence for membership messaging hypotheses and further experiments.

Limitations & responsible interpretation

Rides do not identify individual subscription propensity; marketing benefits are hypothetical until tested.

Source & technical evidence

Follow the repository links to review analytical code, detailed methodology, dashboard captures and reproducibility documentation. Portfolio outcomes are not presented as verified external client results.
