Study 01 / Randomised controlled experiment

Should a redesigned onboarding journey progress to a controlled release?

Design and methods

  • Population: 16,000 fictional customers, assigned 8,000 per group.
  • Primary estimand: intention-to-treat difference in first card payment within seven days.
  • Inference: two-sided 95% Wald interval and pre-randomisation CUPED adjustment.
  • Validation: sample ratio mismatch check, exposure instrumentation, financial and support guardrails.
ControlTreatment

Estimated activation lift: +3.79 pp; 95% CI approximately +2.24 to +5.33 pp, p≈0.0000016.

Business decision and limitations

Consider a guarded rollout candidate only after event-validation and guardrail review; monitor disputes and operational burden, define rollback triggers and repeat where risks remain underpowered. This synthetic fixed-sample demonstration does not certify production readiness, and secondary outcomes are exploratory.

Study 02 / Observational causal inference

Did a selected-cohort product feature change weekly engagement?

Business problem: a non-randomised feature launch cannot be evaluated reliably using before-and-after outcomes alone. We estimate the average treatment-associated change under a parallel-trends assumption.

Identification and implementation

  • 4,500 fictional customers observed for five pre-launch and five post-launch weeks (45,000 customer-weeks).
  • Customer fixed effects through demeaning; common calendar-week effects; standard errors clustered by customer.
  • Balanced panel, binary weekly activity and a linear-probability two-way fixed-effects DiD.
  • Compare pre-treatment trends and investigate exposure selection and concurrent product shocks.
Synthetic point estimate
+5.37 pp

Conditional 95% interval: +3.62 to +7.11 percentage points.

Pretrend-slope diagnostic p = 0.786. A nonsignificant pretrend test does not establish parallel trends.

Decision and limitations

Use this result to prioritise a prospective randomised study where feasible, not to claim proven causality. Group-specific shocks, time-varying selection, comparator contamination, spillovers and changing measurement can invalidate the estimate.

Study 03 / Machine learning & responsible product intervention

Can a churn model support a safe, testable retention strategy?

Evaluation protocol

  • One snapshot per fictional customer, with historical 30/90-day features and a future 30-day churn label.
  • Train on January–April 2026; withhold May for label maturity. Validate on June; withhold July. Test once on August after outcomes mature in September.
  • Compare logistic regression with random forest using validation AUC. Train-only imputation and preprocessing prevent leakage.
  • Measure ROC AUC, PR AUC, Brier score, calibration and concentration at a 10% contact-capacity threshold.
Synthetic untouched holdout
0.799 ROC AUC

PR AUC: 0.216 · Brier score: 0.0525 · Churn prevalence: 6.1%.

Selected model: logistic regression. Ranking churn risk alone does not identify persuadable customers.

Decision and limitations

Do not deploy unsolicited outreach based only on predictions. Define eligibility, opt-outs and harm reviews, then randomise a treatment/holdout group to estimate actual incremental retention and complaints. The model estimates risk, not intervention lift; simulator economics are assumed, not observed.
